# AI GOVERNANCE POLICY

## Chambers & Whitfield LLP — Artificial Intelligence Usage Policy

---

**Policy Owner:** Managing Partner
**Approved By:** Management Board
**Effective Date:** 1 January 2026
**Review Date:** 31 December 2026
**Version:** 1.0
**Classification:** Internal — All Staff

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 This policy sets out the framework for the responsible adoption and use of artificial intelligence ("AI") tools and systems by Chambers & Whitfield LLP (the "Firm") in the delivery of legal services and business operations.

1.2 This policy applies to all partners, solicitors, trainees, paralegals, legal secretaries, and support staff of the Firm, and to any third-party contractors or consultants engaged by the Firm who use AI tools in connection with Firm business.

1.3 The Firm recognises the potential of AI to enhance legal services, improve efficiency, and deliver value to clients, while acknowledging the risks associated with AI use, including risks to accuracy, confidentiality, data protection, and professional obligations.

---

## 2. Regulatory Framework

2.1 The Firm is authorised and regulated by the **Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA)**, SRA Number 654321.

2.2 This policy has been developed with reference to:

- The **SRA Standards and Regulations** (November 2019, as amended), including the SRA Principles and the SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors, RELs and RFLs, and the SRA Code of Conduct for Firms
- The **SRA Technology and AI Guidance** (updated 2025)
- The **UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR)** and the **Data Protection Act 2018**
- The **Equality Act 2010**
- The **UK Government AI Regulation White Paper** (March 2023) and subsequent policy developments
- The **Law Society Practice Note on AI and Legal Services** (2025)

2.3 Nothing in this policy overrides the Firm's obligations under the SRA Standards and Regulations. In the event of any conflict, the SRA Standards and Regulations shall prevail.

## 3. Approved AI Tools

3.1 The Firm has approved the following AI tools for use in the delivery of legal services and business operations:

| Tool | Provider | Use Case | Risk Rating |
|------|----------|----------|-------------|
| LexisNexis+ AI | RELX Group | Legal research, case analysis | Medium |
| CoCounsel | Thomson Reuters | Document review, due diligence | Medium |
| Harvey | Harvey AI | Contract analysis, drafting assistance | Medium |
| Microsoft Copilot for M365 | Microsoft | Email drafting, document summarisation | Low-Medium |
| iManage RAVN | iManage | Document extraction, knowledge management | Low |

3.2 Staff shall not use any AI tool that has not been approved by the Firm's IT Committee and Risk & Compliance Partner without prior written authorisation.

3.3 The use of consumer-grade AI tools (including ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Claude, or similar general-purpose AI assistants) for any matter involving client data, confidential information, or work product is **strictly prohibited** unless specifically approved under clause 3.2.

---

## 4. SRA Competence and Training

4.1 In accordance with **SRA Principle 2** (acting in a way that upholds public trust and confidence in the solicitors' profession) and **SRA Principle 5** (acting with integrity), all fee-earners must maintain competence in the use of AI tools deployed in their practice areas.

4.2 The Firm shall provide the following mandatory training:

| Training Module | Audience | Frequency |
|----------------|----------|-----------|
| AI Fundamentals for Legal Professionals | All fee-earners | Annual |
| AI Tool-Specific Training (per approved tool) | Users of specific tools | On adoption and annual refresher |
| AI Risk and Ethics | All partners and senior associates | Annual |
| AI and Data Protection | All staff | Annual |
| AI Supervision and Review | All supervisors | Annual |

4.3 Fee-earners shall not use an approved AI tool until they have completed the relevant tool-specific training and received confirmation from the Learning & Development team.

4.4 In accordance with the **SRA Competence Statement**, solicitors must be able to:

(a) identify when AI tools are appropriate for a particular task;

(b) critically evaluate AI-generated outputs for accuracy, relevance, and completeness;

(c) recognise the limitations of AI tools, including the risk of hallucination, bias, and outdated information;

(d) exercise independent professional judgment and not delegate decision-making to AI tools.

---

## 5. Human Oversight and Supervision

5.1 All AI-generated outputs used in the delivery of legal services must be reviewed by a qualified solicitor before being relied upon, sent to a client, filed with a court, or incorporated into any legal document.

5.2 The supervising solicitor retains full professional responsibility for the accuracy and quality of any work product, regardless of whether AI tools were used in its preparation.

5.3 AI tools shall be used as assistants, not as substitutes for professional judgment. No AI tool shall be used to make autonomous decisions in relation to client matters.

5.4 Where AI is used for legal research, the supervising solicitor must verify all case citations, statutory references, and legal propositions against primary sources.

---

## 6. Client Disclosure

6.1 The Firm is committed to transparency with clients regarding its use of AI technology.

6.2 **The Firm's standard engagement letter includes a general statement that the Firm may use technology tools, including AI-assisted tools, in the delivery of legal services.** Clients are not required to provide specific consent to the use of AI tools, as the Firm considers that general technology disclosure is sufficient to meet its transparency obligations.

6.3 Where a client specifically requests that AI tools are not used on their matter, the Firm shall comply with that request and record it in the matter management system.

---

## 7. Data Protection and Confidentiality

7.1 All use of AI tools must comply with the Firm's Data Protection Policy and the requirements of the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018.

7.2 Client data and confidential information shall only be processed by AI tools where:

(a) the AI tool has been approved under clause 3 of this policy;

(b) appropriate data processing agreements are in place with the AI tool provider;

(c) the data is processed within the United Kingdom or in a jurisdiction with an adequate level of data protection as determined by the UK Secretary of State;

(d) the processing is necessary and proportionate for the purpose of delivering legal services to the client.

7.3 Staff must not input client data into any AI tool that processes data outside approved environments.

7.4 The Firm shall maintain a record of AI tools used on each matter in the matter management system.

---

## 8. Quality Assurance

8.1 The Firm shall conduct quarterly reviews of AI tool usage to assess quality, accuracy, and compliance with this policy.

8.2 Any errors or issues arising from AI-generated outputs shall be reported to the Risk & Compliance Partner and recorded in the Firm's incident register.

8.3 The Firm shall maintain an AI incident log recording all material errors, near-misses, and complaints arising from the use of AI tools.

---

## 9. Ethical Obligations

9.1 The use of AI tools shall not compromise the Firm's duties under the SRA Principles, including:

(a) the duty to act in the best interests of each client (Principle 7);

(b) the duty of confidentiality;

(c) the duty to act with independence;

(d) the duty not to mislead the court or others.

9.2 Fee-earners must not represent AI-generated work as their own original analysis without review and verification.

9.3 Where AI-generated content is used in court filings, the filing solicitor must be able to vouch for the accuracy and completeness of all content.

---

## 10. Risk Management

10.1 The Firm's Risk & Compliance Partner is responsible for maintaining an AI risk register and for conducting periodic risk assessments in relation to AI tool usage.

10.2 All new AI tools must undergo a risk assessment before approval, covering:

(a) data security and privacy;

(b) accuracy and reliability;

(c) regulatory compliance;

(d) professional indemnity insurance implications;

(e) vendor due diligence.

---

## 11. Governance and Oversight

11.1 The Management Board has overall responsibility for the Firm's AI strategy and governance.

11.2 The IT Committee, chaired by the Managing Partner, is responsible for evaluating, approving, and monitoring AI tools.

11.3 The Risk & Compliance Partner is responsible for ensuring that AI usage complies with the SRA Standards and Regulations and this policy.

11.4 The Data Protection Officer (appointed under Article 37 of the UK GDPR) is responsible for advising on data protection aspects of AI tool usage.

---

## 12. Review

12.1 This policy shall be reviewed annually by the Management Board, or sooner if there are material changes to the regulatory framework, the Firm's AI tool usage, or the risk landscape.
