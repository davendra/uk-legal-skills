# Modern Slavery and Human Trafficking Statement

## Financial Year Ending 31 December 2025

### GlobalTech Supply Ltd

---

## 1. Introduction

This statement is made pursuant to **section 54 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015** (the **"Act"**) and constitutes GlobalTech Supply Ltd's modern slavery and human trafficking statement for the financial year ending 31 December 2025.

This statement sets out the steps that GlobalTech Supply Ltd and its subsidiaries (together, the **"Group"** or **"we"**) have taken and continue to take to ensure that modern slavery and human trafficking are not taking place within our business or supply chains.

The Board of Directors takes a zero-tolerance approach to modern slavery and is committed to acting ethically and with integrity in all of our business dealings and relationships, and to implementing and enforcing effective systems and controls to ensure modern slavery is not taking place anywhere in our business or in any of our supply chains.

## 3. Policies in Relation to Slavery and Human Trafficking

We have implemented the following policies and procedures relevant to the identification and prevention of modern slavery:

### 3.1 Modern Slavery Policy

Our standalone Modern Slavery Policy (last reviewed October 2025) sets out the Group's commitment and the responsibilities of all employees and contractors. It includes:

- A clear definition of modern slavery, servitude, forced labour, and human trafficking
- The Group's zero-tolerance approach
- Procedures for identifying and reporting concerns
- Protections for whistleblowers

### 3.2 Supplier Code of Conduct

Our Supplier Code of Conduct (adopted March 2023, updated September 2025) requires all direct suppliers to:

- Comply with all applicable laws regarding modern slavery and human trafficking
- Not use forced, bonded, indentured, or involuntary labour
- Not use child labour (as defined by the ILO Minimum Age Convention No. 138)
- Ensure that workers' identity documents are not retained
- Ensure that no recruitment fees are charged to workers
- Provide safe and hygienic working conditions
- Pay at least the applicable minimum wage
- Respect workers' right to freedom of association and collective bargaining
- Cascade these requirements to their own suppliers

### 3.3 Whistleblowing Policy

Our Whistleblowing Policy provides a confidential mechanism for employees, agency workers, and contractors to report concerns about modern slavery or any other unethical conduct, without fear of retaliation. Reports can be made to the Group Compliance Officer, via our anonymous third-party whistleblowing hotline (operated by Safecall Ltd), or directly to the Board.

### 3.4 Recruitment and Agency Workers Policy

Our Recruitment and Agency Workers Policy ensures that:

- All recruitment agencies used by the Group are required to confirm compliance with the Modern Slavery Act 2015 and the Gangmasters (Licensing) Act 2004 (where applicable)
- Pre-engagement due diligence is conducted on all staffing agencies
- Right to work checks are performed for all employees and agency workers in accordance with the Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006

## 4. Due Diligence Processes

### 4.1 Supply Chain Due Diligence

We have implemented a supply chain due diligence programme that includes:

**(a) Risk Mapping:** We have mapped our supply chains by country, sector, and commodity to identify areas of elevated modern slavery risk using the Global Slavery Index, the US Department of Labor's List of Goods Produced by Child Labor or Forced Labor, and the Walk Free Foundation's data.

**(b) Supplier Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ):** All Tier 1 suppliers are required to complete an annual SAQ covering labour practices, recruitment procedures, working conditions, and sub-contracting arrangements. In FY 2025, 94% of Tier 1 suppliers completed the SAQ (FY 2024: 89%).

**(c) Supplier Audits:** We conduct risk-based supplier audits, prioritising suppliers in high-risk jurisdictions or sectors. In FY 2025, we conducted 18 on-site audits (FY 2024: 14), including 7 unannounced audits of suppliers in China and Vietnam.

**(d) Contract Clauses:** All new and renewed supplier contracts include modern slavery clauses requiring compliance with the Act, cooperation with audits, and a right to terminate in the event of non-compliance.

**(e) Corrective Action:** Where issues are identified through audits or SAQs, we work with suppliers to implement corrective action plans with defined timescales. In FY 2025, 3 corrective action plans were issued and all were remediated within the agreed timeframes.

### 4.2 Internal Due Diligence

- All new employees undergo right to work verification and employment eligibility checks
- HR conducts periodic reviews of employment practices across the Group
- Procurement team reviews all new supplier engagements for modern slavery risk indicators
- The Group Compliance Officer maintains an ongoing monitoring programme

## 5. Risk Assessment

### 5.1 Areas of Identified Risk

Our risk assessment (last reviewed November 2025) has identified the following areas of elevated risk:

| Risk Area | Risk Level | Mitigation |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Electronics component manufacturing in China (Tier 2) | High | Increased audit frequency, Tier 2 supplier mapping, membership of Responsible Business Alliance (RBA) |
| Logistics and transportation (UK and international) | Medium | Due diligence on haulage subcontractors, driver welfare checks |
| Temporary and agency labour (UK warehousing) | Medium | Pre-engagement due diligence on agencies, regular site visits, direct engagement with agency workers |
| Cleaning and facilities management (UK offices) | Medium-Low | Supplier code of conduct compliance, annual SAQ, contract clauses |
| Raw material sourcing (conflict minerals) | High | Conflict minerals reporting, OECD Due Diligence Guidance compliance, supplier engagement |

### 5.2 Emerging Risks

The Board has noted the following emerging risks for FY 2026:

- Increased sourcing from Vietnam, where labour inspection regimes are less developed
- Ongoing geopolitical disruption affecting supply chain transparency
- Growth in gig economy and platform work arrangements in logistics

## 7. Training

### 7.1 Employee Training

We provide the following modern slavery training:

| Training | Audience | Frequency |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| Modern Slavery Awareness (e-learning module) | All employees | Annual |
| Advanced Modern Slavery and Supply Chain Due Diligence | Procurement, compliance, and HR teams | Annual |
| Identifying Indicators of Modern Slavery | Warehouse, logistics, and facilities staff | Annual |
| Board-level Briefing on Modern Slavery Risks | Board of Directors | Annual (November) |
| Induction training (including modern slavery module) | All new joiners | On joining |

### 7.2 Training Content

Training covers:

- Definition and forms of modern slavery (forced labour, servitude, human trafficking, debt bondage)
- Indicators of modern slavery (e.g., restriction of movement, withholding of documents, excessive working hours, poor living conditions)
- The Group's modern slavery policies and procedures
- How to report concerns (including whistleblowing mechanisms)
- Legal framework (Modern Slavery Act 2015, relevant ILO Conventions)
- Case studies relevant to the technology distribution sector

### 7.3 Training Effectiveness

In FY 2025, 98% of employees completed the mandatory modern slavery awareness training (FY 2024: 95%). Post-training assessments showed an average score of 87% (FY 2024: 83%), indicating improving understanding across the Group.

## 8. Progress and Future Commitments

### 8.1 Progress in FY 2025

Key achievements in FY 2025 include:

- Completed mapping of 85% of Tier 2 supply chain (up from 72% in FY 2024)
- Conducted 18 on-site supplier audits including 7 unannounced audits
- Joined the Responsible Business Alliance (RBA) and adopted the RBA Code of Conduct as the baseline for supplier engagement in electronics manufacturing
- Updated the Supplier Code of Conduct to include enhanced provisions on recruitment fees and worker grievance mechanisms
- Achieved 94% Tier 1 supplier SAQ completion rate (up from 89%)
- Delivered Board-level training on emerging modern slavery risks

### 8.2 Commitments for FY 2026

For the financial year ending 31 December 2026, we commit to:

- Achieving 100% Tier 1 supplier SAQ completion
- Mapping 95% of Tier 2 supply chain
- Commencing systematic mapping of Tier 3 suppliers for critical commodities
- Conducting a minimum of 20 on-site supplier audits, including at least 10 in high-risk jurisdictions
- Piloting worker voice technology (anonymous worker surveys) at 5 high-risk supplier sites
- Publishing a standalone conflict minerals report
- Reviewing and updating the modern slavery risk assessment to reflect emerging risks in Vietnam and gig economy logistics
- Achieving 100% employee training completion
