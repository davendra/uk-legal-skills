## 1. Introduction and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This Anti-Money Laundering and Know Your Client Policy (the **"Policy"**) sets out the procedures and controls that Henderson & Cole Solicitors LLP (the **"Firm"**) has implemented to prevent the Firm from being used to facilitate money laundering or terrorist financing and to ensure compliance with all applicable anti-money laundering legislation and regulation.

### 1.2 Regulatory Framework

This Policy has been prepared in accordance with:

- The Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (**"POCA"**), as amended by the Serious Organised Crime and Police Act 2005 and the Criminal Finances Act 2017
- The Terrorism Act 2000 (**"TA 2000"**), as amended
- The Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 (SI 2017/692) (**"MLR 2017"**), as amended by the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2019 (SI 2019/1511) and the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2022 (SI 2022/860)
- The Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) Standards and Regulations, in particular the Code of Conduct for Firms
- The Legal Sector Affinity Group (LSAG) Anti-Money Laundering Guidance for the Legal Sector (approved by HM Treasury)

### 1.3 Scope

This Policy applies to:

- All partners, members, solicitors, legal executives, paralegals, trainees, and other fee earners
- All support staff, including accounts, reception, and administrative staff
- All temporary workers, contractors, and consultants engaged by the Firm

## 3. Firm-Wide Risk Assessment

### 3.1 Obligation

In accordance with Regulation 18 of the MLR 2017, the Firm has carried out and maintains a written firm-wide risk assessment identifying and assessing the risks of money laundering and terrorist financing to which the Firm is subject.

### 3.2 Risk Factors

The risk assessment considers the following factors:

**Client Risk Factors:**
- Type of client (individuals, companies, trusts, partnerships, PEPs)
- Client location (UK, EEA, high-risk third countries per FATF)
- Business relationship purpose and nature
- Client's source of funds and source of wealth

**Service/Product Risk Factors:**
- Types of legal services provided (conveyancing, company/commercial, trusts, litigation)
- Complexity and value of transactions
- Involvement of client money
- Matters involving overseas elements

**Geographic Risk Factors:**
- Countries identified by FATF as high-risk or under increased monitoring
- Countries subject to UK financial sanctions (per OFSI Consolidated List)
- Countries with high levels of corruption (per Transparency International CPI)

**Delivery Channel Risk Factors:**
- Non-face-to-face client relationships
- Referrals from intermediaries
- Use of nominee arrangements

### 3.3 Annual Review

The firm-wide risk assessment is reviewed **annually** by the MLRO and approved by the partners. The most recent review was completed on 15 December 2025.

## 4. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

### 4.1 When CDD Must Be Conducted

In accordance with Regulation 27 of the MLR 2017, the Firm must conduct CDD:

(a) When establishing a business relationship;

(b) When carrying out an occasional transaction amounting to EUR 15,000 or more (whether in a single transaction or in linked transactions);

(c) When there is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing, regardless of the value;

(d) When there are doubts about the veracity or adequacy of documents or information previously obtained for CDD purposes.

### 4.2 Standard CDD Requirements

For all clients, the Firm must:

**(a) Identify the client** and verify their identity on the basis of documents, data, or information obtained from a reliable and independent source:

- **Individuals:** Full name, date of birth, residential address. Verified by current passport or photocard driving licence, plus a recent utility bill, bank statement, or council tax bill (dated within the last 3 months).

- **Companies (UK):** Full name, registered number, registered office address, and the names of the board of directors. Verified by Companies House search (including confirmation statement, persons with significant control register) and, where appropriate, the company's certificate of incorporation.

- **Companies (Overseas):** As above, plus certificate of incorporation or equivalent from the relevant jurisdiction, evidence of registration, and constitutional documents.

**(b) Identify the beneficial owner(s)** and take reasonable measures to verify their identity:

- For companies: any individual who ultimately owns or controls more than 25% of the shares or voting rights, or who otherwise exercises control over the management of the company (Regulation 5 MLR 2017).

- For trusts: the settlor, trustee(s), beneficiaries (or class of beneficiaries), protector, and any other individual who exercises ultimate effective control.

**(c) Assess and, as appropriate, obtain information on the purpose and intended nature** of the business relationship or occasional transaction.

**(d) Conduct ongoing monitoring** of the business relationship, including scrutiny of transactions to ensure they are consistent with the Firm's knowledge of the client, their business, and risk profile.

### 4.3 Timing

CDD must be completed **before** the establishment of a business relationship or the carrying out of an occasional transaction. In accordance with Regulation 30, verification of identity may be completed during the establishment of the business relationship where:

(a) this is necessary so as not to interrupt the normal conduct of business; and

(b) there is little risk of money laundering or terrorist financing;

provided verification is completed as soon as practicable after initial contact.

### 4.4 Failure to Complete CDD

Where the Firm is unable to complete CDD in accordance with this section, the Firm must **not** establish a business relationship or carry out the transaction, must terminate any existing business relationship, and must consider making a SAR to the NCA.

## 5. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

### 5.1 When EDD Is Required

In accordance with Regulation 33 of the MLR 2017, the Firm must apply EDD measures and enhanced ongoing monitoring in the following situations:

(a) **Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs):** Where the client, or the beneficial owner of the client, is a PEP (as defined in Regulation 35(12)), a family member of a PEP, or a known close associate of a PEP;

(b) **High-risk third countries:** Where the client or any party to the transaction is established or resides in a country identified by FATF as having strategic AML/CFT deficiencies;

(c) **Complex or unusually large transactions:** Transactions which are complex, unusually large, or have an unusual pattern and have no apparent economic or lawful purpose;

(d) **Other high-risk situations:** Any situation presenting a higher risk of money laundering or terrorist financing as identified by the Firm's risk assessment.

### 5.2 EDD Measures

EDD measures may include (as appropriate):

- Obtaining additional identification documents from independent sources
- Obtaining information on the source of funds and source of wealth
- Conducting enhanced internet and media searches
- Obtaining senior management approval to establish or continue the business relationship
- Increasing the frequency and intensity of ongoing monitoring
- Requiring the first payment to be made from a UK-regulated bank account in the client's name

### 5.3 PEPs

For PEPs, the Firm must additionally:

(a) Have senior management approval for establishing or continuing the business relationship;

(b) Take adequate measures to establish the source of wealth and the source of funds involved in the relationship or transaction; and

(c) Conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring of the relationship.

## 6. Sanctions Screening

### 6.1 OFSI Obligations

In accordance with the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 and applicable UK sanctions regulations, the Firm must not deal with or make funds or economic resources available to any **designated person** listed on the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (**"OFSI"**) Consolidated List.

### 6.2 Screening Process

(a) All new clients and their beneficial owners must be screened against the OFSI Consolidated List at the point of client onboarding.

(b) All existing clients must be rescreened whenever the OFSI Consolidated List is updated (the Firm subscribes to OFSI's e-alert service for notifications of updates).

(c) Screening must also be conducted against the HM Treasury financial sanctions targets list, the EU Consolidated List (where relevant to the transaction), and the UN Security Council Consolidated List.

(d) The Firm uses the electronic screening tool provided by Comply Advantage for automated screening, supplemented by manual checks where necessary.

### 6.3 Positive Matches

Where a potential match is identified:

(a) The matter must be immediately escalated to the MLRO;

(b) No funds may be transferred or services provided pending the MLRO's determination;

(c) If the match is confirmed, the MLRO must report to OFSI immediately (failure to comply is a strict liability criminal offence under the relevant sanctions regulations); and

(d) The Firm must freeze any funds or assets held and not proceed with the matter.

## 7. Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)

### 7.1 Internal Reporting

Any member of the Firm who knows or suspects, or has reasonable grounds for knowing or suspecting, that a person is engaged in money laundering or terrorist financing must report the matter to the MLRO **immediately** using the Firm's internal SAR form.

### 7.2 MLRO Assessment

Upon receipt of an internal SAR, the MLRO will:

(a) Consider whether the report gives rise to knowledge or suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing;

(b) Make further inquiries if necessary (without tipping off the subject);

(c) Decide whether an external SAR should be made to the NCA; and

(d) Record the decision and reasons in the MLRO's SAR register.

### 7.3 External Reporting to the NCA

Where the MLRO determines that a SAR should be made externally, the MLRO will submit the SAR to the NCA via the NCA's SAR Online system. Where **consent** is required to proceed with a transaction (a Defence Against Money Laundering, or "DAML" request), the MLRO will include a request for an appropriate consent decision.

### 7.4 Consent Regime

(a) Where appropriate consent is requested, the Firm must not proceed with the relevant transaction until one of the following occurs:

- The NCA grants consent;
- 7 working days have elapsed from the date of the SAR without the NCA refusing consent (deemed consent under s.335(2) POCA); or
- If consent is refused, 31 calendar days have elapsed from the date of the refusal without a restraint order or other court intervention (moratorium period under s.335(3) POCA).

(b) The MLRO will maintain a record of all consent requests and outcomes.

### 7.5 Tipping Off

It is a criminal offence under sections 333A and 333D of POCA and section 21D of TA 2000 to disclose to any person that a SAR has been made or that an investigation is being or may be contemplated. All staff must be aware of this prohibition.

## 8. Record-Keeping

### 8.1 Requirements

In accordance with Regulation 40 of the MLR 2017, the Firm must retain:

(a) Copies of all CDD and EDD documents and information (or references to the evidence of identity obtained) for a period of **5 years** from the date on which the business relationship ends or the occasional transaction is completed;

(b) Records of all transactions carried out during the business relationship, sufficient to permit reconstruction of individual transactions, for a period of **5 years** from the date of the transaction;

(c) Records of all internal and external SARs, including the MLRO's assessment and decision, for a period of **5 years** from the date of the report.

### 8.2 Storage

All AML records are stored securely within the Firm's case management system (Proclaim by Eclipse) with access restricted to authorised personnel. Physical documents are stored in locked filing cabinets with access controlled by the MLRO.

The MLRO maintains records of all training delivered, including the date, content, attendees, and trainer. Completion of training is monitored and non-attendance is escalated to the relevant partner.

## 10. Governance and Review

### 10.1 Accountability

The MLRO reports to the partners' meeting on AML compliance matters at least quarterly, covering:

- Number of internal SARs received and external SARs submitted
- Outcome of sanctions screening
- CDD compliance rates
- Training completion rates
- Any regulatory developments or guidance changes

### 10.2 Annual Review

This Policy, together with the Firm's risk assessment and AML procedures, is reviewed **annually** by the MLRO and approved by the partners. The review considers:

- Changes in legislation, regulation, and guidance
- Changes in the nature and volume of the Firm's work
- Findings from any internal or external audit
- Any relevant enforcement actions or published guidance from the SRA, NCA, or FATF
- The effectiveness of existing controls

### 10.3 Breach Reporting

Any breach of this Policy must be reported to the MLRO immediately. The MLRO will investigate the breach, take remedial action, and, where required, report the matter to the SRA.
